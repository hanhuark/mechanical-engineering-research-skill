# Han Hu Manuscript Revision

Use `mechanical-engineering-research` in Han Hu manuscript mode.

1. Inspect the current manuscript source, figures and captions, tables, equations, cited evidence, reviewer comments, and available data before editing.
2. Read `han-hu-research-style.md`, `paper-writing-style.md`, `manuscript-revision-submission.md`, and `han-hu-style-calibration-protocol.md`.
3. Retrieve two to four private corpus excerpts matched to the section’s genre and rhetorical function. Prefer accepted or author-approved after-text; preserve each exemplar’s evidence status.
4. Diagnose paragraph logic, physical framing, figure roles, claim strength, definitions, conditions, evidence boundaries, redundancy, and reader clarity before sentence-level polishing.
5. For abstracts, introductions, and conclusions, check that the narrative separates the existing gap from the work's actual contribution. State the consequence of the gap, what the work does with its data or model, what it finds, and how the finding should be used. Do not let a known limitation become the manuscript's main conclusion.
6. Correct substantive problems through analysis, evidence, definitions, or explicit limitations where required. Do not merely weaken prose to hide a validity gap.
7. Preserve values, units, equations, citations, sample sizes, uncertainty statements, definitions, and evidence class. Flag any proposed change that would alter them.
8. Return the revised text, a change log separating substantive corrections from style edits, unresolved blockers, and a style-deviation audit.

Expected output:

- revised manuscript text
- substantive-versus-style change log
- unresolved technical or evidence blockers
- reader-clarity and narrative audit for abstracts, introductions, or conclusions
- style-deviation audit
