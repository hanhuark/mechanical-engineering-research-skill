# Author Interview Case Study: One Results Section, Eight Rounds

This record follows the author interview in `author-guided-ai-writing.md` through its first live use, on the results section of an unpublished journal manuscript in pool boiling. The manuscript extended an accepted conference paper whose prose the author had approved. The section discussed one figure, and an AI agent had drafted its prose. The author judged the draft accurate but hard to read: it carried trivial detail, internal case identifiers, defensive qualifications, and no clear message.

The record keeps the process and omits the data, the findings, and anything that identifies the study. It is a process example, not a result.

## Starting material

- The current manuscript source, the figure designed by the author, and the archived analysis code and data.
- The approved conference paper, used as calibration.
- The author's request: improve one results section and its figure discussion to the level that researchers in the field will understand and appreciate.

## What each round surfaced

| Round | Questions | What the answers changed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intake | None; facts extracted from the source and the conference paper | A fact sheet with every value marked "to confirm", and a list of three objects that each had several names in the draft |
| 1. Story | Key message, reader, gap and barrier, claim ceiling, carrying figure, with the conference paper's message quoted in the first question | The author explained that the paper had been reframed from the conference topic to a new central phenomenon, and that the introduction's gap statement was left over from the old framing |
| 2. Follow-ups | Only the missing element of each answer | A typo in the author's new gap sentence, the barrier that kept the gap open, and the reason one test series could be run at only one condition (a fact the manuscript never stated) |
| 3. Final follow-ups | Direction of the effect, competing explanation from the conference paper, metric versus motivation, claim verb | The author identified the true control variable; found that the stated motivation had never been analyzed and asked for the analysis; chose "is consistent with" and rejected the trailing hedge; and rejected an AI-written conservative sentence that the data contradicted |
| Between rounds | Analyses run from the archived data | The requested analysis supported the motivation, and a check of the controlled series showed the contradicted sentence was indeed wrong |
| 4. Figure | Carrying subfigure, observation, mechanism, physical meaning of a fitted constant, metric naming | The author wanted a physical meaning for the fitted constant and asked for literature candidates; he redefined the metric so that it rises with the effect |
| Between rounds | Literature candidates tested with the constant held fixed; references verified in the originals; machine drawing used for a heater property | One classical candidate matched the fitted value within its interval with no fitted temperature; hydrodynamic candidates failed outright |
| 5 to 7. Decisions | Which candidate, which plotted quantity, which mechanisms, what moves to the supplement, where the new analysis goes | Each answered in one or two lines; editorial questions came with recommendations and were mostly accepted |
| 8. Drafting | Two sections and two supplementary sections drafted one unit at a time | Placeholders kept for a figure choice and a missing citation; a correction the author wanted to keep went to a short main-text discussion plus a supplementary section |

## Lessons that changed the protocol

1. **Quote the earlier approved version.** The conference paper exposed the reframing and a competing explanation in the first round.
2. **Load the author's style references before proposing any wording.** A record entry drafted with a colon clause, `so` as a connective, a needless hyphenated compound, and context the reader had not yet received cost a round, although every rule was already documented.
3. **Fix the analysis, not the wording.** Three of the most useful outcomes were analyses the interview revealed or the author requested: a severity measure that had never been checked, physically derived values for a fitted constant, and a heater property computed from the drawing.
4. **Check hedges against the data.** A default conservative sentence denied an effect that a controlled series in the same paper showed. A limitation states what the evidence does not separate, once, and is checked before it is written.
5. **Measure the draft.** The first draft averaged about 22 words per sentence, above the author's measured target and above a draft he had already rejected. A scripted check caught it before the author saw it.
6. **Answered rounds are confirmation.** A separate request to confirm the record would have added a round with no new information.

## Outcome

The revised results section is about 20 percent shorter than the AI draft. It has no colons, semicolons, case identifiers, or negated results. Every number in it traces to the decision record or to a saved analysis script. The author's assessment: "This is much better! This example demonstrates that the workflow works very well."
